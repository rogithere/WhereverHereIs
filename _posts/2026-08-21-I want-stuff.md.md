I like stuff. I want stuff. Wanting stuff is the primary preoccupation of our species. It is impossible to discard. There’s our first problem. Secondly when we conceptualize this process as having wants (or needs) there is a ‘wanter(A)’ and the thing(B) that is wanted. What is the relationship between A & B ? If we forbid some claim about the individual psychology of A then we must provide a more general explanation of A & B. This makes sense because of the generality of this experience. So commonplace explanations won’t do.   
Allow me to bring in ‘new’ description of the above phenomenon. New for this blog. If I say we have desires and it attaches to things in this world, this tells us a little more about how A & B interact. This obviously makes many many assumptions about the nature of A & B. Lets set that aside for now. We’ll deal with that at some later time. 

Now when we say we have desires which attach to things, then a human being would have infinite desires. This is because of the following reasons -   
1. There are infinite things in the world

2. Human desire is infinite
    
    At some point in our lives we learn how to desire things, channel that desire in a healthy and productive ways etc. It’s one of the reasons why teenagers suffer so much with a breakup or their friends not speaking to them. They simply do not know how to deal with not getting what they want. Of course adults suffer too for much the same reasons but they have access to mitigating resources. Human beings are if anything limited creatures. There are hard biological constraints on what we can and cannot do. If that’s the case how can this limited, finite human being have infinite desires. Where is it stored ? Where is it located ? Our new description is in trouble looks like! Well, we can save it by making the desires go from infinite to one. Then our new description becomes - We have one desire. It latches on to things in this world. How many things ? As many as there exist in this world ? How ? Through attention.   
    I’d like to use the common notion of attention and use it to describe the dynamics of desire. I’m out of coffee so I’ll do that in the next post. See ya then. Take care.
